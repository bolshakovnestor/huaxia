饮食文化
yǐnshí wénhuà

Cuisine

The Chinese say: 民以食为天mín yǐ shí wéi tiān (“For the people, food is heaven”). Every region has its own flavour.

North and south

Wheat or rice

Traditionally 南米北面nán mǐ běi miàn (“rice in the south, flour in the north”): in the north — 面条miàntiáo (noodles), 馒头mántou (steamed buns), 饺子jiǎozi (dumplings); in the south — 米饭mǐfàn (cooked rice).

The Eight Great Cuisines 八大菜系

The classification

鲁菜Lǔcài (Shandong), 川菜Chuāncài (Sichuan), 粤菜Yuècài (Guangdong (Cantonese)), 苏菜Sūcài (Jiangsu), 闽菜Mǐncài (Fujian), 浙菜Zhècài (Zhejiang), 湘菜Xiāngcài (Hunan), 徽菜Huīcài (Anhui). The map below shows their regions.

Flavours

Sichuan — 麻辣málà (numbing and spicy); Guangdong — freshness and delicacy, 点心diǎnxin (dim sum) with 早茶zǎochá (morning tea); Hunan — 香辣xiānglà (fragrant and hot); Jiangsu and Zhejiang — slightly sweet.

Famous dishes

Peking duck

北京烤鸭Běijīng kǎoyā (Peking duck) is served with thin pancakes, spring onion and sweet bean sauce.

Hot pot

火锅huǒguō (“fire pot”): diners cook ingredients themselves at the table in a simmering broth.

Sichuan classics

麻婆豆腐mápó dòufu (mapo tofu), 宫保鸡丁gōngbǎo jīdīng (Kung Pao chicken), 回锅肉huíguōròu (twice-cooked pork).

Noodles

兰州牛肉面Lánzhōu niúròu miàn (Lanzhou beef noodles) are pulled by hand (拉面lāmiàn (hand-pulled noodles)).

Table etiquette

Chopsticks

筷子kuàizi (chopsticks): don't stick them upright in rice — it resembles incense sticks at a funeral.

Shared dishes

Dishes are placed in the centre and shared; often the host pays for everyone (请客qǐngkè (to treat)).

Toast

干杯gānbēi (“bottoms up”) is the most common toast.
