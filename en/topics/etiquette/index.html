礼仪
lǐyí

Etiquette and communication

Understanding 面子miànzi (“face”, reputation) and 关系guānxi (connections) helps in study, business and friendship with Chinese people.

Key concepts

Face

面子miànzi (“face”): don't criticise someone in public — it makes them 丢面子diū miànzi (“lose face”). Praise means 给面子gěi miànzi (“giving face”).

Connections

关系guānxi (a network of personal ties and mutual obligations) is an important part of social and business life.

Modesty

A compliment is often answered with 哪里哪里nǎli nǎli (“oh, not at all”) or 过奖了guòjiǎng le (“you flatter me”).

Greetings and forms of address

Politely

您好nín hǎo (Hello (respectful)); 您nín (you (polite)) is the polite form of 你nǐ (you).

Forms of address

Surname + title: 王老师Wáng lǎoshī (Teacher Wang), 李经理Lǐ jīnglǐ (Manager Li). Chinese surnames come first.

Everyday greeting

吃了吗？Chī le ma? (“Have you eaten?”) — a friendly formula like “how are you”.

Gifts

With both hands

Gifts, 名片míngpiàn (business cards) and documents are given and received with both hands.

Taboos

Clocks are not given as gifts: 送钟sòng zhōng (giving a clock) sounds like 送终sòngzhōng (attending someone's death). Pears are not shared: 分梨fēn lí (splitting a pear) ~ 分离fēnlí (separation).

Red envelopes

The amount in a 红包hóngbāo (red envelope) should be even, ideally with 6 or 8, avoiding 4.
