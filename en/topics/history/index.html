History and dynasties

Over three millennia of written history: from the legendary Xia to the modern republic. Knowing the order of dynasties helps with everything from poetry to porcelain.

Key turning points

First unification (221 BC)

秦始皇Qín Shǐhuáng (the First Emperor of Qin) united the warring states and standardised writing, measures and coinage. The European name “China” probably derives from Qin.

Han — “the Han people”

The 汉Hàn (Han) dynasty (206 BC – 220 AD) gave its name to the main ethnic group and the language: 汉人Hànrén (Han Chinese), 汉语Hànyǔ (Chinese language).

Tang — the golden age

唐Táng (Tang) (618–907): the flowering of poetry, the cosmopolitan capital 长安Cháng'ān (Chang'an), the Silk Road.

End of the empire

The 辛亥革命Xīnhài Gémìng (Xinhai Revolution) (1911) overthrew the Qing dynasty; on 1 January 1912 the 中华民国Zhōnghuá Mínguó (Republic of China) was proclaimed.

The Silk Road

丝绸之路

The 丝绸之路Sīchóu zhī Lù (Silk Road) was a network of trade routes linking China, Central Asia and the Mediterranean. The term was coined by the German geographer Ferdinand von Richthofen in 1877.
