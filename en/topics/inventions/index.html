四大发明
sì dà fāmíng

The Four Great Inventions

Paper, printing, gunpowder and the compass — the “Four Great Inventions” that changed the world.

The four inventions

Paper 造纸术

造纸术zàozhǐshù (papermaking): the official 蔡伦Cài Lún (Cai Lun) improved the technique in 105 AD and presented it to the emperor. Archaeologists have found even older paper from the Western Han.

Printing 印刷术

雕版印刷diāobǎn yìnshuā (woodblock printing) dates to the Tang; the Diamond Sutra of 868 is the oldest dated printed book. 活字印刷huózì yìnshuā (movable-type printing) was invented by 毕昇Bì Shēng (Bi Sheng) in the 1040s (as recorded by 沈括Shěn Kuò (Shen Kuo)).

Gunpowder 火药

火药huǒyào (“fire medicine”) was discovered by Taoist alchemists seeking an elixir of immortality. The earliest gunpowder formulas appear in the military treatise 武经总要Wǔjīng Zǒngyào (“Wujing Zongyao”) (1044).

Compass 指南针

指南针zhǐnánzhēn (“south-pointing needle”). Shen Kuo described the magnetic needle (1088); compasses were used for navigation by the early 12th century. Earlier still there was the 司南sīnán (lodestone “south-pointing spoon”).

Other achievements

Silk

丝绸sīchóu (silk) has been produced in China since the Neolithic; the secret was kept for a long time.

Seismoscope

张衡Zhāng Héng (Zhang Heng) built a 地动仪dìdòngyí (seismoscope) in 132 AD.

Abacus

算盘suànpán (the Chinese abacus); knowledge and practices of zhusuan are on the UNESCO list (2013).
