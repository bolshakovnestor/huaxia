Festivals

Most traditional festivals follow the lunisolar 农历nónglì (agricultural (lunar) calendar), so their dates change every year.

Spring Festival (Chinese New Year)

春节 — 1st day of the 1st month

春节Chūnjié (Spring Festival) falls between 21 January and 20 February. In 2024 UNESCO inscribed it on the list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Customs

On 除夕Chúxī (New Year's Eve) the family gathers for the 年夜饭niányèfàn (reunion dinner); in the north people make 饺子jiǎozi (dumplings). Children receive 红包hóngbāo (red envelopes with money), doors are decorated with 春联chūnlián (New Year couplets), and 鞭炮biānpào (firecrackers) are set off.

Upside-down 福

The character 福fú (good fortune) is hung upside down: “upside down” 倒dào (inverted) sounds like 到dào (arrived) — “fortune has arrived”.

Greetings

新年快乐Xīnnián kuàilè (Happy New Year!), 恭喜发财Gōngxǐ fācái (Wishing you prosperity!), 拜年bàinián (paying New Year visits).

Other festivals

元宵节 — 15th day of the 1st month

The 元宵节Yuánxiāojié (Lantern Festival) closes the New Year period: people eat 汤圆tāngyuán (sweet rice balls) and solve 灯谜dēngmí (lantern riddles).

清明节 — 4 or 5 April

The 清明节Qīngmíngjié (Qingming (Tomb-Sweeping) Festival) honours ancestors: 扫墓sǎomù (sweeping the graves) and spring outings 踏青tàqīng (“treading the green”). Its date follows the solar calendar.

端午节 — 5th day of the 5th month

The 端午节Duānwǔjié (Dragon Boat Festival) is linked to the poet 屈原Qū Yuán (Qu Yuan). People hold 赛龙舟sài lóngzhōu (dragon-boat races) and eat 粽子zòngzi (sticky rice in bamboo leaves). UNESCO — 2009.

七夕 — 7th day of the 7th month

七夕Qīxī (Qixi, the “Chinese Valentine's Day”) — the legend of 牛郎织女Niúláng Zhīnǚ (the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl), who meet once a year on a bridge of magpies.

中秋节 — 15th day of the 8th month

中秋节Zhōngqiūjié (Mid-Autumn Festival): people admire the full moon (赏月shǎngyuè (moon-gazing)), eat 月饼yuèbing (mooncakes) and recall the goddess 嫦娥Cháng'é (Chang'e). The central idea is 团圆tuányuán (family reunion).

重阳节 — 9th day of the 9th month

重阳节Chóngyángjié (Double Ninth Festival): 登高dēnggāo (climbing to a height), chrysanthemums; today also Seniors' Day.

国庆节 — 1 October

A national holiday; together with weekends it forms the 黄金周huángjīnzhōu (“Golden Week”).
