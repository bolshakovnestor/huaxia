儒释道
Rú Shì Dào

Philosophy and religion

Three teachings — Confucianism, Buddhism and Taoism — have coexisted for centuries, shaping the Chinese worldview, ethics and art.

Confucianism 儒家

Confucius

孔子Kǒngzǐ (Confucius, “Master Kong”) (551–479 BC) came from the state of Lu (today's Shandong). His sayings are collected in the 论语Lúnyǔ (“Analects”).

Core values

仁rén (benevolence), 义yì (righteousness, duty), 礼lǐ (ritual, propriety), 孝xiào (filial piety), 智zhì (wisdom), 信xìn (trustworthiness).

Influence

From the Han onward Confucianism underpinned state education and the 科举kējǔ (imperial examinations), which lasted until 1905.

Taoism 道家 / 道教

Laozi and Zhuangzi

老子Lǎozǐ (Laozi) is the traditional author of the 道德经Dàodéjīng (“Tao Te Ching”); 庄子Zhuāngzǐ (Zhuangzi) wrote parables, including the butterfly dream.

Ideas

The 道dào (Way) is the source of all things; 无为wúwéi (non-action, acting without forcing); 阴阳yīnyáng (yin and yang) — complementary opposites.

Philosophy and religion

One distinguishes the philosophical school 道家Dàojiā (Taoist school) from the religion 道教Dàojiào (Taoism as a religion), with temples, priests and sacred mountains (such as Wudang).

Buddhism 佛教

Arrival from India

佛教Fójiào (Buddhism) reached China around the 1st century AD. In the 7th century the monk 玄奘Xuánzàng (Xuanzang) travelled to India for sutras — the basis of the novel “Journey to the West”.

Chan

禅宗Chánzōng (Chan school) is the Chinese school of meditative Buddhism that became Zen in Japan. The 少林寺Shàolín Sì (Shaolin Monastery) was founded in 495.

Today

Officially recognised religions

Five religions are officially registered in the PRC: Buddhism, Taoism, Islam, Catholicism and Protestantism. Ancestor veneration is also widespread.
