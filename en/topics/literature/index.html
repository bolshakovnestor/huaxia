Literature

From ancient classics to a Nobel laureate, Chinese literature has one of the longest continuous traditions in the world.

The classics

Key texts

诗经Shījīng (“Classic of Poetry”) — the oldest poetry collection (11th–6th c. BC); 论语Lúnyǔ (the “Analects” of Confucius); 道德经Dàodéjīng (“Tao Te Ching”); 孙子兵法Sūnzǐ Bīngfǎ (“The Art of War” by Sunzi).

Tang and Song poetry

Great poets

李白Lǐ Bái (Li Bai) (701–762), the “Immortal Poet”; 杜甫Dù Fǔ (Du Fu) (712–770), the “Sage Poet”; 苏轼Sū Shì (Su Shi) (1037–1101), also known as Su Dongpo — poet, calligrapher and even the namesake of the dish “Dongpo pork”.

The Four Great Classical Novels 四大名著

Romance of the Three Kingdoms

三国演义Sānguó Yǎnyì (“Romance of the Three Kingdoms”) — 罗贯中Luó Guànzhōng (Luo Guanzhong), 14th c. The wars of Wei, Shu and Wu.

Water Margin

水浒传Shuǐhǔ Zhuàn (“Water Margin”) — attributed to 施耐庵Shī Nài'ān (Shi Nai'an). 108 noble outlaws.

Journey to the West

西游记Xīyóu Jì (“Journey to the West”) — attributed to 吴承恩Wú Chéng'ēn (Wu Cheng'en), 16th c. The Monkey King 孙悟空Sūn Wùkōng (Sun Wukong) escorts the monk 唐僧Táng Sēng (Tang Seng) to India.

Dream of the Red Chamber

红楼梦Hónglóu Mèng (“Dream of the Red Chamber”) — 曹雪芹Cáo Xuěqín (Cao Xueqin), 18th c. The summit of the Chinese novel.

Modern times

Lu Xun

鲁迅Lǔ Xùn (Lu Xun), the founder of modern Chinese literature; 狂人日记Kuángrén Rìjì (“A Madman's Diary”) (1918).

Nobel Prize

莫言Mò Yán (Mo Yan) received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2012.

Chengyu 成语

Four-character idioms

画蛇添足huà shé tiān zú (“drawing legs on a snake” — overdoing it), 对牛弹琴duì niú tán qín (“playing the zither to a cow” — talking to the wrong audience), 守株待兔shǒu zhū dài tù (“waiting by a stump for a hare” — relying on luck), 入乡随俗rù xiāng suí sú (“when in Rome, do as the Romans do”).
