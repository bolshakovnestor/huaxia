茶文化
chá wénhuà

Tea culture

China is the homeland of tea. In 2022 UNESCO inscribed China's traditional tea-processing techniques and associated social practices on its Intangible Heritage list.

History

The Classic of Tea

陆羽Lù Yǔ (Lu Yu) wrote the 茶经Chájīng (“Classic of Tea”) during the Tang dynasty (8th century) — the world's first book about tea.

The word “tea”

Words like Russian and Ukrainian “chai” come from the northern 茶chá (tea), while English “tea” comes from the southern Fujian (Min) pronunciation “te”.

Six categories 六大茶类

By degree of oxidation

绿茶lǜchá (green), 白茶báichá (white), 黄茶huángchá (yellow), 青茶qīngchá (oolong (blue-green)), 红茶hóngchá (“red” = black tea in the West), 黑茶hēichá (dark, post-fermented). See the interactive scale below.

Famous teas

Green

西湖龙井Xīhú Lóngjǐng (Longjing, Hangzhou), 碧螺春Bìluóchūn (Biluochun, Suzhou).

Oolong

铁观音Tiěguānyīn (Tieguanyin, Anxi), 大红袍Dàhóngpáo (Da Hong Pao, Wuyi Mountains) — both from Fujian.

Dark and black

普洱Pǔ'ěr (Pu'er, Yunnan); 祁门红茶Qímén hóngchá (Keemun, Anhui).

The tea ceremony

Gongfu cha

功夫茶gōngfu chá (“tea made with skill”) — brewing in small portions in a 盖碗gàiwǎn (lidded bowl) or a 紫砂壶zǐshāhú (Yixing clay teapot).

A gesture of thanks

When someone refills your cup, you can lightly tap the table with two fingers — 叩指礼kòuzhǐlǐ (finger-tapping thanks).

Teahouse

The 茶馆cháguǎn (teahouse) is a traditional place to socialise, especially in Chengdu.
