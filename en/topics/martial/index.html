Martial arts and traditional medicine

Martial arts and traditional medicine are part of the cultural heritage, closely tied to philosophy.

Wushu 武术

Wushu and kung fu

武术wǔshù (martial arts); the Western word kung fu comes from 功夫gōngfu (skill achieved through hard work).

Shaolin and Wudang

少林Shàolín (Shaolin) (Henan) — “external” styles; 武当Wǔdāng (Wudang) (Hubei) — “internal” styles linked to Taoism.

Taijiquan

太极拳tàijíquán (tai chi) — smooth movements based on the idea of yin and yang; UNESCO — 2020.

Bruce Lee

李小龙Lǐ Xiǎolóng (Bruce Lee, “Little Dragon”) popularised Chinese martial arts in world cinema.

Traditional medicine 中医

A cultural phenomenon

中医Zhōngyī (traditional Chinese medicine) is based on ideas of 气qì (qi, vital energy), yin–yang and the five elements. Scientific evidence of effectiveness is lacking for most of its practices.

Acupuncture

针灸zhēnjiǔ (acupuncture and moxibustion) was inscribed by UNESCO as intangible heritage (2010) — as a cultural practice.

Yangsheng

养生yǎngshēng (“nourishing life”) — an everyday health culture: warm water, morning exercises in parks, tea.
