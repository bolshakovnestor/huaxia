艺术
yìshù

Arts

Calligraphy, ink painting, opera, paper-cutting and porcelain are hallmarks of Chinese aesthetics.

Calligraphy 书法

The Four Treasures of the Study

文房四宝wénfáng sìbǎo (the Four Treasures of the Study): 笔bǐ (brush), 墨mò (ink), 纸zhǐ (paper), 砚yàn (inkstone).

Script styles

篆书zhuànshū (seal script), 隶书lìshū (clerical script), 楷书kǎishū (regular script), 行书xíngshū (running script), 草书cǎoshū (cursive “grass” script).

The “Sage of Calligraphy”

王羲之Wáng Xīzhī (Wang Xizhi) (4th c.) wrote the celebrated 兰亭集序Lántíng Jí Xù (“Preface to the Orchid Pavilion Poems”) (353 AD). Chinese calligraphy has been on the UNESCO list since 2009.

Painting 国画

Genres

山水画shānshuǐhuà (“mountains and water” — landscape), 花鸟画huāniǎohuà (flowers and birds), 人物画rénwùhuà (figure painting).

Techniques

工笔gōngbǐ (“meticulous brush” — detailed style) and 写意xiěyì (“sketching the idea” — freehand style).

The Four Gentlemen

四君子sì jūnzǐ (the Four Gentlemen): 梅méi (plum blossom (resilience)), 兰lán (orchid (refinement)), 竹zhú (bamboo (integrity)), 菊jú (chrysanthemum (seclusion)).

Peking opera 京剧

Origins

京剧Jīngjù (Peking opera) took shape in Beijing after 1790, based on troupes from Anhui and Hubei. UNESCO — 2010.

Role types

生shēng (male roles), 旦dàn (female roles), 净jìng (“painted faces”), 丑chǒu (clown).

Face painting

脸谱liǎnpǔ (opera face make-up): red stands for loyalty and courage (e.g. 关羽Guān Yǔ (Guan Yu)), black for uprightness, white for treachery (classically 曹操Cáo Cāo (Cao Cao)).

Folk arts

Paper-cutting

剪纸jiǎnzhǐ (red paper-cuts) decorate windows at festivals. UNESCO — 2009.

Chinese knot

The 中国结Zhōngguó jié (Chinese decorative knot) symbolises luck and unity.

Musical instruments

古琴gǔqín (guqin, seven-string zither) (UNESCO), 古筝gǔzhēng (guzheng), 二胡èrhú (erhu, two-string fiddle), 琵琶pípa (pipa, lute).

Porcelain 瓷器

“china” = porcelain

In English, “china” also means porcelain. 景德镇Jǐngdézhèn (Jingdezhen) in Jiangxi is the “porcelain capital”; the famous 青花瓷qīnghuācí (blue-and-white porcelain) matured under the Yuan.
