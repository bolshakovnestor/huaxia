Landmarks and UNESCO heritage

As of 2025 China has 60 UNESCO World Heritage Sites — the second-highest number in the world after Italy.

The first sites (1987)

The Great Wall

长城Chángchéng (“Long Wall”) — a system of fortifications built over more than 2,000 years; the best-known sections date from the Ming. A saying: 不到长城非好汉bú dào Chángchéng fēi hǎohàn (“You're not a true hero until you've been to the Great Wall”).

The Forbidden City

故宫Gùgōng (the Forbidden City, “Old Palace”) (Beijing) was built in 1406–1420; home to 24 emperors of the Ming and Qing. Today it is the 故宫博物院Gùgōng Bówùyuàn (Palace Museum).

The Terracotta Army

The 兵马俑Bīngmǎyǒng (terracotta warriors and horses) are part of the mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang near Xi'an; discovered by farmers in 1974.

Mogao Caves

The 莫高窟Mògāo Kū (Mogao Caves) near 敦煌Dūnhuáng (Dunhuang) hold Buddhist murals from the 4th to 14th centuries on the Silk Road.

Mount Tai

泰山Tài Shān (Mount Tai) is the most revered of the 五岳Wǔ Yuè (Five Great Mountains); a mixed (cultural and natural) site.

Other iconic sites

Beijing

天坛Tiāntán (Temple of Heaven), 颐和园Yíhéyuán (Summer Palace), 北京中轴线Běijīng Zhōngzhóuxiàn (Beijing Central Axis) (2024).

Gardens and towns

苏州古典园林Sūzhōu gǔdiǎn yuánlín (Classical Gardens of Suzhou), 平遥古城Píngyáo gǔchéng (Pingyao), 丽江古城Lìjiāng gǔchéng (Lijiang), 西湖Xī Hú (West Lake).

Nature

黄山Huáng Shān (Huangshan), 九寨沟Jiǔzhàigōu (Jiuzhaigou), 武陵源Wǔlíngyuán (Wulingyuan (Zhangjiajie)).

Engineering

The 大运河Dà Yùnhé (Grand Canal) — the longest artificial waterway of the ancient world (UNESCO 2014).

The newest

The 西夏陵Xīxià Líng (Western Xia Imperial Tombs) in Ningxia — the 60th site (2025).
